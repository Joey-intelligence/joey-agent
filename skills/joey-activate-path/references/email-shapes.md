# Email shapes

Use these examples to adapt a Joey draft. The names, companies and facts are
examples only. In a real email, use only facts from the Joey report or from the
member.

A good request for an introduction:

- Is short: 60 to 150 words.
- Says how the member knows the introducer, in one line.
- Gives one fact from the report about how the next two people on the selected path know each other.
- Gives one clear reason for the meeting.
- Makes the ask easy to refuse.
- Has a short note that the introducer can forward as it is.

For a longer path, ask the recipient for help with the next hop. For example,
for You → David → Mira → Sarah, ask David for an introduction to Mira and explain
why you want to reach Sarah. Do not claim that David knows Sarah.

## Good examples

### Good 1: ask a former colleague (Strong hop)

```
Subject: Intro to Sarah Johnson at Acme?

Hi David,

I hope all is well since our Orbit days. I saw that you worked with
Sarah Johnson on the security team at Northwind. Sarah now leads
security at Acme.

We help security teams cut alert noise, and I think a 20-minute call
can help her team. Would you be open to an intro? If it is not a good
fit, no problem.

A short note to forward:
"Dana Levi runs sales at Brightline. They help security teams cut
alert noise. Dana asked for 20 minutes with you."

Thanks,
Dana
```

Why it works: one real tie, one reason, an easy way to say no, and a note
to forward.

### Good 2: REACH_OUT to the target

The next person after the member is the target, so the member writes to the
target.

```
Subject: Northwind security team, and a question

Hi Sarah,

We were both on the Northwind security team in 2021, on different
projects. I now run sales at Brightline. We help security teams cut
alert noise.

Do you have 20 minutes in the next two weeks? If the timing is wrong,
I understand.

Best,
Dana
```

Why it works: it starts with the real shared past and asks for one small
thing.

### Good 3: ask a teammate who is on the path

The member is not on the path. The first person on the path is a teammate.

```
Subject: Can you introduce me to Tom Lee at Northwind?

Hi Ben,

Joey shows that you and Tom Lee worked together at Globex from 2018
to 2020. Tom is now the CISO at Northwind, one of our top accounts.

Would you be willing to introduce us by email? I can send you a short
note to forward.

Thanks,
Dana
```

Why it works: it gives the teammate the fact from the report and a clear ask.

## Bad examples

### Bad 1: generic

```
Subject: Quick favor

Hi David,

Hope you're well! I'd love to connect with Sarah. Could you introduce
us? Thanks so much!
```

Why it fails: no reason for the meeting, no tie, and no note to forward. The
introducer must do all the work.

### Bad 2: pushy

```
Subject: Need this intro by Friday

David,

I need an intro to Sarah by Friday. Please send it today and copy me.
I will follow up tomorrow if I do not hear back.
```

Why it fails: it puts pressure on the introducer, sets a deadline and gives no
easy way to say no.

### Bad 3: invented facts

```
Subject: Intro to Sarah

Hi David,

Since you and Sarah were roommates at Stanford, and she told you that
her team has budget this quarter, could you introduce us?
```

Why it fails: the Joey report does not say this. Invented facts can harm the
member, the introducer and Joey. Use only facts from the report or from the
member.
